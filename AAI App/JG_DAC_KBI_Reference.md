## THE 5 INSPIRE JOY COMPETENCIES

| Code | Full Name | Core Idea |
|------|-----------|-----------|
| DSP | Drive Superior Performance | Entrepreneurial, goal-focused, data-driven |
| WTP | Win Through People | Earns commitment, coaches, holds accountability with empathy |
| LFDV | Learn from Different Views | Continuously learns, speaks up, changes stance on better evidence |
| MaD | Make a Difference | Leads innovation, takes initiative, drives positive change |

## RATING SCALE — DETAILED GUIDANCE

| Level | Label | When to Use |
|-------|-------|-------------|
| 1 | Does Not Demonstrate | Multiple missed opportunities; behaviors opposite to what's expected; intervention needed |
| 2 | Inconsistently Demonstrates | Demonstrates in some moments but with visible gaps or inconsistency; minimal negative impact |
| 3 | Consistently Demonstrates | Regularly and reliably demonstrates the behavior across the activity; meets standard |
| 4 | Exceeds Expectations | Multiple commendable, above-standard moments with clear positive impact on others or outcomes |

**Calibration notes:**
- Level 3 is the expected standard for a strong performer, not a ceiling.
- An overall competency rating is holistic — not an average of KBI ratings. One KBI rated 4 does not make the overall a 4.
- If a candidate demonstrates Level 4 on most KBIs with consistent positive impact throughout, the overall may be 4.
- If a candidate misses 1–2 KBIs but shows solid 3s elsewhere, the overall is typically 2–3 depending on gap severity.
- Never rate above 2 on a KBI where transcript evidence is absent or purely inferred.

## DSP — DRIVE SUPERIOR PERFORMANCE
*"We are entrepreneurial; we collaborate across networks to realize the company's goals."*

### DSP in Analysis Presentation (AP) — Accelerate & First Loyalty

**KBI 1: Communicates clear recommendations and rationale aligned to business priorities**
- L1: Recommendations are vague, unsupported, or not clearly linked to the business problem
- L2: Recommendations are present but the rationale is incomplete or partially disconnected from business priorities
- L3: Gives clear, specific recommendations with logical rationale aligned to the business context and data provided
- L4: Recommendations are prioritized, compelling, and explicitly linked to strategic business impact; anticipates implications
- *Key Progression:* From vague suggestions → to clear, evidence-backed recommendations tied to business value

**KBI 2: Develops strong strategies and practical actions to improve business performance**
- L1: No strategies offered, or strategies are generic and impractical
- L2: Strategies are present but lack specificity, sequencing, or practical feasibility
- L3: Develops clear, structured action plans with realistic steps and responsible parties
- L4: Strategies are innovative, specific, and account for implementation realities; goes beyond the obvious
- *Key Progression:* From generic ideas → to actionable, structured plans with clear ownership

**KBI 3: Considers customer impact and commercial implications in decisions**
- L1: No reference to customer or commercial implications
- L2: Mentions customers or commercial impact but does not integrate it into recommendations
- L3: Consistently factors in customer perspective and commercial viability when presenting solutions
- L4: Proactively reframes recommendations through a customer-commercial lens; explicitly balances both angles
- *Key Progression:* From internal-only view → to integrated customer and commercial thinking

**KBI 4: Recognizes risks and opportunities early and recommends timely business action**
- L1: No risks or opportunities identified; no contingencies
- L2: Identifies some risks or opportunities but without recommended action
- L3: Clearly flags risks and opportunities with corresponding recommended actions
- L4: Anticipates second-order risks, surfaces non-obvious opportunities, and proposes proactive mitigations
- *Key Progression:* From reactive identification → to proactive early-warning with actionable responses

### DSP in Group Exercise (GE) — Accelerate

**KBI 1: Sets a clear direction and drives the group toward a high-quality strategic output**
- L1: Does not contribute to setting direction; follows others passively or derails focus
- L2: Occasionally steers the group but without sustained commitment or impact
- L3: Actively and consistently helps the group maintain focus and move toward a quality recommendation
- L4: Takes clear leadership in setting direction; elevates the quality of the group's output noticeably
- *Key Progression:* From passive participation → to active, direction-setting contribution

**KBI 2: Develops strong strategies, structures the discussion, and pushes for concrete decisions**
- L1: Does not contribute to structuring the discussion; avoids or deflects decision-making moments
- L2: Offers some structure or pushes for decisions inconsistently
- L3: Structures the discussion effectively and advocates for concrete, prioritized decisions
- L4: Drives the group from abstract discussion to crisp, well-structured decisions with clear logic
- *Key Progression:* From passive → to structured contribution that moves the group to action

**KBI 3: Grounds recommendations in data from the case and advocates for prioritized, actionable outcomes**
- L1: Recommendations are not grounded in case data; speaks in generalities
- L2: References data occasionally but recommendations are not consistently evidence-based
- L3: Consistently anchors recommendations in case data and advocates for a clear, prioritized outcome
- L4: Uses data to challenge weak assumptions, strengthens the group's logic, and models analytical rigor
- *Key Progression:* From data-absent → to evidence-anchored advocacy for prioritized outcomes

---

### DSP in CBI — Accelerate

**KBI 1: Drives results with business impact**
- L1: Story lacks specific actions or measurable business outcomes
- L2: Story has some actions and results but business impact is vague or indirect
- L3: Shares a clear story of specific actions leading to concrete, measurable business results
- L4: Story demonstrates exceptional business acumen; outcomes are significant and explicitly tied to strategy
- *Key Progression:* From vague efforts → to specific actions with measurable business outcomes

**KBI 2: Sets direction and leads execution**
- L1: No evidence of leading a project; describes following or being assigned tasks
- L2: Led a project but without clear direction-setting, standards, or follow-through
- L3: Describes leading a project with clear goals, standards set for the team, and sustained follow-through
- L4: Story shows how they navigated complexity, adjusted direction when needed, and delivered exceptional results
- *Key Progression:* From doing the work → to leading with direction, standards, and accountability

---

### DSP in NEG — First Loyalty

**KBI 1: Identifies the most important issue and prioritizes action to protect the client relationship**
- L1: Misses the core issue or addresses low-priority concerns first
- L2: Identifies the issue but action is not clearly prioritized or decisive
- L3: Quickly zeroes in on the most critical issue and takes clear, prioritized action to address it
- L4: Demonstrates exceptional issue diagnosis; addresses root cause while managing immediate impact
- *Key Progression:* From scattered response → to focused, prioritized action on the most critical issue

**KBI 2: Provides practical, well-structured solutions with clear rationale**
- L1: Solutions are vague, impractical, or not explained
- L2: Offers some solutions but without clear rationale or structured presentation
- L3: Presents well-structured, practical solutions with explicit rationale linked to the client's situation
- L4: Solutions are creative, specific, and compellingly framed; anticipates objections and addresses them proactively
- *Key Progression:* From vague offers → to structured, rationale-backed solutions

**KBI 3: Balances customer needs, commercial impact, and company interests in decisions**
- L1: Focuses only on one dimension (e.g., only appeasement or only company protection)
- L2: Attempts to balance but skews heavily toward one dimension; trade-offs are not explicit
- L3: Explicitly acknowledges and balances customer needs with commercial and company implications
- L4: Finds a creative solution that advances all three dimensions simultaneously; frames it compellingly
- *Key Progression:* From one-dimensional view → to integrated three-way balancing

**KBI 4: Recognizes opportunities to strengthen the long-term partnership beyond issue resolution**
- L1: Focuses only on fixing the problem; no forward-looking partnership thinking
- L2: Mentions the relationship in passing but does not take concrete partnership-building action
- L3: After resolving the issue, actively identifies and pursues a partnership-strengthening opportunity
- L4: Frames the resolution as a demonstration of partnership value; proposes concrete next steps for the relationship
- *Key Progression:* From damage control → to partnership deepening

## WTP — WIN THROUGH PEOPLE
*"We earn commitment from our people. We hold each other accountable for performance with empathy and care."*

### WTP in Group Exercise (GE) — Accelerate

**KBI 1: Ensures all voices are heard and creates a safe environment for contributions**
- L1: Dominates the conversation or ignores quieter members; does not invite input
- L2: Occasionally invites input but does not follow through or acknowledge contributions
- L3: Consistently includes all members; acknowledges contributions and creates space for quieter voices
- L4: Actively creates a psychologically safe environment; draws out quieter members with specific, thoughtful invitations
- *Key Progression:* From self-focused → to group-inclusive facilitation

**KBI 2: Rallies the group around a shared goal and maintains group energy and direction**
- L1: Does not contribute to group cohesion; may create tension or confusion
- L2: Occasionally re-focuses the group but energy or direction is not sustained
- L3: Consistently helps the group stay motivated and aligned to a shared outcome
- L4: Elevates group energy at critical moments; reframes conflict as shared purpose; inspires commitment
- *Key Progression:* From neutral presence → to active group motivator

**KBI 3: Helps the group stay on track and monitors progress toward the activity goal**
- L1: Does not contribute to time or progress management; may contribute to going off-track
- L2: Flags time or progress occasionally but without sustained follow-through
- L3: Consistently monitors progress and helps the group course-correct when needed
- L4: Proactively manages structure and pacing; ensures quality checkpoints are met with minimal disruption
- *Key Progression:* From passive → to proactive progress management

---

### WTP in Coaching Roleplay (CR) — Accelerate

**KBI 1: Listens first, acknowledges the coachee's perspective, and creates a safe space**
- L1: Jumps straight to feedback or solutions without listening; dismisses or minimizes the coachee's feelings
- L2: Listens briefly but moves to advice before fully acknowledging the coachee's situation
- L3: Opens by listening, acknowledges feelings and perspective, and creates a safe space before offering feedback
- L4: Demonstrates deep empathy; names unspoken emotions; the coachee visibly feels heard and safe
- *Key Progression:* From telling → to listening and acknowledging first

**KBI 2: Acknowledges strengths and frames improvement as achievable; rebuilds confidence where needed**
- L1: Focuses only on gaps; coachee confidence is diminished
- L2: Mentions strengths but framing is imbalanced or the improvement ask feels like a criticism
- L3: Balances recognition of strengths with clear improvement framing; coachee feels capable and motivated
- L4: Reframes the gap as a growth opportunity in a way that genuinely inspires; improvement feels exciting, not punitive
- *Key Progression:* From deficit-focused → to strength-anchored improvement framing

**KBI 3: Sets specific, concrete expectations for performance improvement with agreed timelines**
- L1: No specific expectations set; conversation ends without clarity on what improvement looks like
- L2: Expectations are set but vague, one-sided, or without agreed timelines
- L3: Establishes specific, agreed expectations with clear timelines and success measures
- L4: Co-creates expectations with the coachee; commitments feel jointly owned and personally meaningful
- *Key Progression:* From vague asks → to specific, jointly owned expectations with timelines

**KBI 4: Uses a coaching approach — asks rather than tells — to build the coachee's own thinking**
- L1: Instructs and directs throughout; the coachee is passive
- L2: Asks some questions but reverts to telling when the coachee struggles
- L3: Consistently uses open questions to surface the coachee's own thinking and solutions
- L4: Masterfully scaffolds the coachee's thinking; the coachee arrives at insights independently with minimal prompting
- *Key Progression:* From directive telling → to coaching through questions

---

### WTP in CBI — Accelerate

**KBI 1: Manages a difficult or underperforming team member with care and clear consequences**
- L1: Story shows avoidance, pure discipline, or zero empathy — no balance
- L2: Story has some empathy or some accountability but not both
- L3: Story demonstrates balanced handling: empathy for the person + clear expectations and consequences
- L4: Story shows sophisticated navigation of a complex interpersonal situation with lasting positive outcome
- *Key Progression:* From one-dimensional response → to balanced empathy + accountability

**KBI 2: Develops someone's skills or capability through coaching or mentoring**
- L1: No evidence of deliberate capability development; gives tasks but not growth
- L2: Describes supporting someone but without structured coaching or meaningful development intent
- L3: Shares a specific example of deliberately developing a person's capability with follow-through
- L4: Story demonstrates deep investment in another person's growth; measurable capability improvement resulted
- *Key Progression:* From task delegation → to deliberate, sustained capability development

**KBI 3: Keeps a team motivated and aligned during a difficult or high-pressure situation**
- L1: Team fell apart or candidate did not actively address the challenge to motivation/alignment
- L2: Took some actions to support the team but impact on motivation or alignment was limited
- L3: Took clear, deliberate actions that sustained team motivation and alignment through the challenge
- L4: Story shows inspirational leadership; team performance was elevated despite the adversity
- *Key Progression:* From reactive management → to proactive team inspiration during difficulty

---

### WTP in NEG — First Loyalty

**KBI 1: Builds rapport and manages the conversation with empathy, professionalism, and respect**
- L1: Comes across as dismissive, defensive, or adversarial; rapport is damaged
- L2: Manages the conversation professionally but without real empathy or warmth
- L3: Demonstrates genuine empathy while maintaining professionalism; rapport is built and sustained
- L4: Creates a noticeably positive emotional dynamic; the client moves from distress to trust within the conversation
- *Key Progression:* From transactional → to genuinely empathetic and rapport-building

**KBI 2: Rebuilds confidence and gains commitment toward sustained partnership**
- L1: Client confidence is not rebuilt; the conversation ends with unresolved doubt
- L2: Some confidence is restored but the commitment to continue the partnership is weak or conditional
- L3: Effectively rebuilds confidence and secures a genuine, specific commitment to move forward
- L4: Client confidence is fully restored and the candidate has elevated the client's trust in the partnership
- *Key Progression:* From damage limitation → to active confidence restoration

**KBI 3: Sets clear expectations and addresses performance concerns through defined accountability**
- L1: Performance concerns are not addressed; no expectations or accountability framework set
- L2: Raises the concern but accountability structure is vague or one-sided
- L3: Sets clear expectations for both parties with a defined accountability structure going forward
- L4: Co-creates accountability in a way the client embraces; frames it as partnership governance
- *Key Progression:* From avoidance → to shared, clearly defined accountability

**KBI 4: Builds client buy-in through shared ownership and commitment to agreed actions**
- L1: Client is passive; agreed actions are imposed rather than co-created
- L2: Some buy-in achieved but ownership of actions is primarily one-sided
- L3: Achieves genuine shared ownership; client actively commits to agreed next steps
- L4: Client advocates for the solution; feels like a co-author of the path forward
- *Key Progression:* From compliance → to genuine shared ownership and commitment

## LFDV — LEARN FROM DIFFERENT VIEWS
*"We constantly learn from different ideas to improve. We respect others enough to tell them what we think."*

### LFDV in Group Exercise (GE) — Accelerate

**KBI 1: Challenges assumptions and raises critical questions to improve discussion quality**
- L1: Does not question assumptions; accepts what others say uncritically or stays silent
- L2: Raises a question or challenges an assumption once but without follow-through or impact
- L3: Consistently raises useful questions or challenges assumptions in ways that improve group thinking
- L4: Reframes the problem or uncovers a critical assumption that fundamentally improves the group's output
- *Key Progression:* From passive acceptance → to active, constructive challenge of assumptions

**KBI 2: Synthesizes multiple viewpoints and uses data to strengthen group recommendations**
- L1: Does not integrate others' views; advocates only for own perspective
- L2: References others' points occasionally but synthesis is shallow or incomplete
- L3: Actively synthesizes diverse perspectives and data points to strengthen the group's recommendation
- L4: Creates a higher-quality synthesis than any individual contribution; makes the sum greater than the parts
- *Key Progression:* From single-perspective advocacy → to multi-view synthesis that elevates the output

**KBI 3: Demonstrates openness to changing views when presented with better arguments or data**
- L1: Holds rigidly to original position regardless of new evidence or arguments
- L2: Shows some openness but ultimately reverts to original position without fully acknowledging the better argument
- L3: Visibly and genuinely updates position when presented with a stronger argument or data point
- L4: Models intellectual humility; acknowledges the better argument explicitly and builds on it
- *Key Progression:* From rigidity → to genuine, visible openness to updating one's view

---

### LFDV in Coaching Roleplay (CR) — Accelerate

**KBI 1: Asks open-ended questions and listens actively before offering solutions**
- L1: Jumps to solutions or conclusions without asking; does not listen to understand
- L2: Asks some questions but reverts to telling before the coachee's full picture is understood
- L3: Consistently asks open-ended questions and listens to build understanding before offering input
- L4: Demonstrates sophisticated active listening; reflects back nuance and asks questions that open new insight
- *Key Progression:* From solution-first → to question-and-listen-first approach

**KBI 2: Maintains clear feedback and expectations even when the coachee challenges or deflects**
- L1: Backs down from feedback when the coachee pushes back; abandons key messages under pressure
- L2: Holds position initially but softens to the point of losing the core message when challenged
- L3: Maintains clear, respectful feedback even when the coachee challenges, deflects, or becomes emotional
- L4: Navigates pushback with skill; acknowledges the coachee's reaction while firmly and kindly maintaining the message
- *Key Progression:* From capitulation under pressure → to respectful firmness under challenge

**KBI 3: Adapts the coaching plan when given new or unexpected information by the coachee**
- L1: Ignores new information; sticks rigidly to a pre-planned approach
- L2: Acknowledges new information but does not meaningfully adjust the coaching approach
- L3: Adjusts the coaching plan appropriately when the coachee reveals new context or information
- L4: Uses the new information to reframe the entire conversation in a more relevant and useful direction
- *Key Progression:* From rigid pre-planned approach → to responsive, adaptive coaching

---

### LFDV in CBI — Accelerate

**KBI 1: Sought and meaningfully incorporated feedback from others**
- L1: No story of seeking feedback; story is about receiving unsolicited criticism at best
- L2: Sought feedback but the incorporation into behavior or action was minimal
- L3: Sought specific feedback and made clear, deliberate changes as a result
- L4: Story shows a culture of feedback-seeking; changes made improved outcomes measurably
- *Key Progression:* From feedback-avoidance → to proactive feedback-seeking with meaningful change

**KBI 2: Navigated a conflict of views using evidence or diverse perspectives**
- L1: Avoided the conflict or resolved it by imposing own view without genuine engagement
- L2: Engaged with the conflict but resolution was more about compromise than the best answer
- L3: Used data, evidence, or diverse perspectives to reach a better outcome than either position alone
- L4: Story shows genuine intellectual wrestling with competing views; the outcome was clearly superior because of the process
- *Key Progression:* From avoidance or imposition → to evidence-driven resolution

**KBI 3: Changed stance based on a better idea or new information**
- L1: Cannot recall changing a stance; story shows defending original view throughout
- L2: Changed position but only partially or after significant delay; credit to the better idea is indirect
- L3: Clearly and authentically describes changing stance when presented with a better idea or evidence
- L4: Story shows that changing their mind led to a better outcome; they credit the other person or new data explicitly
- *Key Progression:* From entrenched positions → to genuine, acknowledged stance change

---

### LFDV in NEG — First Loyalty

**KBI 1: Asks thoughtful questions and listens actively to understand the client's concerns**
- L1: Talks more than listens; misses or dismisses the client's stated concerns
- L2: Asks some questions but moves to resolution before fully understanding the client's position
- L3: Consistently asks open, thoughtful questions and demonstrates genuine understanding before responding
- L4: Uncovers unexpressed concerns through skilled questioning; client feels deeply understood
- *Key Progression:* From assumption-making → to thorough, empathetic understanding

**KBI 2: Shares views clearly and provides constructive challenge while maintaining openness**
- L1: Either avoids sharing views or asserts them in a way that shuts down dialogue
- L2: Shares views but without constructive framing; or backs down entirely when challenged
- L3: Clearly shares own perspective and provides constructive challenge while remaining genuinely open
- L4: Models intellectual courage and openness simultaneously; both positions improve through the dialogue
- *Key Progression:* From avoidance or imposition → to constructive, open dialogue

**KBI 3: Uses the client's perspective to improve decisions and next steps**
- L1: Client's input does not influence the candidate's proposals or decisions
- L2: Acknowledges the client's perspective but does not integrate it into solutions
- L3: Explicitly draws on the client's perspective to improve or reframe the proposed solution
- L4: The solution is noticeably better because of the client's input; candidate credits the client's view
- *Key Progression:* From one-way solution delivery → to client-informed co-creation

## MaD — MAKE A DIFFERENCE
*"We take the lead and make a difference through continuous innovation that drives positive change."*

### MaD in Analysis Presentation (AP) — Accelerate & First Loyalty

**KBI 1: Suggests practical improvements to current business challenges**
- L1: No improvement ideas offered; describes the problem without proposing solutions
- L2: Offers generic improvement ideas without practical specificity
- L3: Proposes specific, grounded improvements that are feasible within the business context
- L4: Improvement ideas are innovative yet practical; they would meaningfully shift the business trajectory
- *Key Progression:* From problem description → to specific, actionable improvement proposals

**KBI 2: Takes initiative in addressing issues despite incomplete information**
- L1: Waits for more information before acting; avoids taking a position under uncertainty
- L2: Acknowledges the need to act but hedges heavily; limited decisiveness shown
- L3: Takes a clear, reasoned position and proposes action despite ambiguity or incomplete data
- L4: Demonstrates confident initiative; frames the approach to managing uncertainty explicitly and compellingly
- *Key Progression:* From waiting for certainty → to decisive action under ambiguity

**KBI 3: Tests alternatives, adapts recommendations, and incorporates broader perspectives**
- L1: Presents one recommendation without considering alternatives or acknowledging trade-offs
- L2: Mentions an alternative but does not meaningfully evaluate it or incorporate broader perspectives
- L3: Evaluates multiple alternatives, incorporates relevant perspectives, and defends the chosen recommendation
- L4: Demonstrates rigorous scenario testing; shows how the recommended path is robust to different assumptions
- *Key Progression:* From single-option thinking → to multi-scenario evaluation with a defensible choice

---

### MaD in CBI — Accelerate

**KBI 1: Champions a new idea or process change and overcomes initial pushback**
- L1: No story of championing change; describes going along with the status quo
- L2: Proposed a new idea but abandoned it when challenged; or implemented without meaningful resistance
- L3: Championed a change despite pushback; persisted and secured adoption through influence
- L4: Story shows strategic navigation of resistance; the change had lasting positive organizational impact
- *Key Progression:* From compliance → to persistent, influential change advocacy

**KBI 2: Created space for experimentation or learning in their team or organization**
- L1: No evidence of enabling others' experimentation or learning; was the sole change agent
- L2: Created space for experimentation once or in a limited way without systemic impact
- L3: Deliberately created conditions for team experimentation or learning; took steps to protect the space
- L4: Built a culture of learning and experimentation; the approach outlasted the individual initiative
- *Key Progression:* From solo innovation → to enabling a culture of experimentation

## ET — ESTABLISH TRUST
*"We build the best team and trust them to make decisions and take calculated risks."*

### ET in Analysis Presentation (AP) — Accelerate & First Loyalty

**KBI 1: Makes clear and timely decisions, takes accountability, and demonstrates ownership**
- L1: Avoids making a clear decision; hedges, defers, or blames external factors
- L2: Makes a decision but without clear ownership or with significant qualification that undermines commitment
- L3: Makes a clear, timely decision and takes explicit ownership of the recommendation and its implications
- L4: Decision-making is confident and decisive; candidate frames accountability as a personal commitment
- *Key Progression:* From hedging → to clear, owned decision-making

**KBI 2: Balances short-term decisions with long-term business impact and protection of company reputation**
- L1: Focuses only on the immediate problem; no consideration of long-term or reputational implications
- L2: Mentions long-term or reputational considerations but does not integrate them into the recommendation
- L3: Explicitly balances short-term action with long-term business health and reputational stewardship
- L4: Frames the recommendation compellingly as both immediately effective and long-term protective
- *Key Progression:* From short-term fix → to integrated short- and long-term stewardship

**KBI 3: Encourages cross-functional collaboration and supports decisions using relevant data and expertise**
- L1: Makes recommendations in isolation; no mention of involving others or using external expertise
- L2: Mentions collaboration in passing but does not demonstrate it in the recommendation or analysis
- L3: Actively draws on cross-functional perspectives and data sources in developing the recommendation
- L4: Cross-functional collaboration is a feature of the solution design; brings in specific teams or data compellingly
- *Key Progression:* From solo → to deliberately cross-functional approach

---

### ET in Coaching Roleplay (CR) — Accelerate

**KBI 1: Takes clear ownership of the situation and commits to specific follow-through**
- L1: Passes accountability back to the coachee entirely; no ownership or follow-through commitments made
- L2: Makes vague follow-through commitments without specificity or clear ownership
- L3: Clearly owns the coaching conversation and commits to specific follow-through actions (check-in dates, support)
- L4: Frames own accountability as integral to the coachee's success; models the ownership they are asking for
- *Key Progression:* From shifting accountability → to modeling ownership through specific commitments

**KBI 2: Creates a clear accountability framework that empowers the coachee to self-manage**
- L1: No accountability structure created; conversation ends without a tracking mechanism
- L2: Establishes some accountability but it is externally imposed, not empowering
- L3: Co-creates a structured accountability framework (milestones, check-ins) that the coachee can own
- L4: The framework empowers the coachee to self-monitor and self-correct; manager role is supporting, not policing
- *Key Progression:* From external control → to coachee-owned accountability structure

**KBI 3: Closes with clear mutual commitments and shared understanding of next steps**
- L1: Conversation ends without agreed next steps; both parties are unclear on what happens next
- L2: Some next steps are mentioned but commitments are one-sided or unspecific
- L3: Closes the session with explicit, mutual commitments and a shared understanding of what success looks like
- L4: Summary of commitments is co-constructed; both parties leave with complete clarity and confidence
- *Key Progression:* From open-ended close → to crisp, mutually owned commitments

---

### ET in CBI — Accelerate

**KBI 1: Made a hard judgment call and took full accountability for the result**
- L1: Story avoids the hardest part of the decision; outcome is managed but not owned
- L2: Made the decision but accountability for the outcome is partial or conditional
- L3: Describes a genuinely difficult judgment call and takes clear, explicit accountability for the result — positive or negative
- L4: Story demonstrates moral courage; the candidate owned an imperfect outcome and learned from it without deflecting
- *Key Progression:* From avoidance or blame → to full ownership of a hard call and its outcome

**KBI 2: Delegated meaningfully and created a follow-through structure**
- L1: No meaningful delegation; did the work themselves or only delegated trivial tasks
- L2: Delegated but without a clear development intent or follow-through structure
- L3: Delegated a meaningful responsibility with clear expectations, development intent, and a follow-through structure
- L4: Delegation visibly grew the other person; candidate describes a deliberate and sustained empowerment approach
- *Key Progression:* From doing-it-all → to deliberate, developmental delegation with accountability

---

### ET in NEG — First Loyalty

**KBI 1: Takes clear ownership of issues and follows through on commitments**
- L1: Deflects, minimizes, or shifts blame for the issue; no ownership taken
- L2: Acknowledges the issue but ownership is partial; follow-through commitments are vague
- L3: Takes explicit, unqualified ownership of the issue and makes specific follow-through commitments
- L4: Ownership is immediate and unconditional; commitments are detailed, credible, and proactively communicated
- *Key Progression:* From deflection → to clear, unconditional ownership with specific commitments

**KBI 2: Balances immediate client recovery with long-term business impact and company reputation**
- L1: Focuses solely on appeasing the client without considering business or reputational implications
- L2: Attempts to balance but the trade-offs between recovery and long-term impact are not made explicit
- L3: Explicitly weighs immediate recovery against long-term business health and reputational stewardship
- L4: Finds a solution that is both immediately satisfying to the client and strategically sound for the company
- *Key Progression:* From appeasement → to integrated short- and long-term stewardship

**KBI 3: Enables confident, well-informed decisions through clear ownership and accountability**
- L1: Decision-making is muddled; ownership is unclear and next steps are ambiguous
- L2: Some clarity provided but decision ownership and accountability framework are incomplete
- L3: Creates a clear decision framework with explicit ownership and accountability for all parties
- L4: Structures the conversation so that both parties leave with complete confidence, clarity, and commitment
- *Key Progression:* From ambiguity → to structured, confident decision ownership

## DAC REPORT WRITING GUIDELINES

### Report Structure

1. **Candidate & Assessment Information** — Name, position applied for, program, assessment date, assessors
2. **Purpose of the Assessment** — One paragraph on the DAC's role in the talent pipeline decision
3. **Instruments Used** — One short paragraph per activity conducted (AP, GE, CR, CBI, or NEG). State what the activity measures and how it was administered.
4. **Summary of Scores** — Table of all competencies assessed, with a rating per activity and an overall rating. Color-coded bars: Red=L1, Orange=L2, Green=L3, Dark Green=L4.
5. **Per-Competency Sections** (one per competency) — See format below
6. **Executive Summary** — Career aspiration, overall recommendation, development priorities

---

### Per-Competency Section Format

**[Competency Name]**

**Behavioral Observations from Structured Exercises**
Write 2–4 sentences per activity that assessed this competency. Cite specific, observable behaviors. Do not copy-paste evidence verbatim from the rating form — synthesize and elevate into a narrative. Use past tense. Attribute behaviors to the candidate by name.

*Example:*
> In the Group Exercise, Ana consistently anchored the group's discussion to the case data, redirecting peers when the conversation drifted into anecdote. She structured the final recommendation with clear logic and explicitly assigned accountability for each action item, elevating the group's output quality.

**Competency-Based Interview (SBII)**
Write the CBI section in SBII format: Situation → Behavior → Impact → Intent.
- **Situation:** 1–2 sentences establishing context (what was happening, what the challenge was)
- **Behavior:** 2–3 sentences describing what the candidate specifically did (actions, not traits)
- **Impact:** 1–2 sentences on what resulted — quantify if possible
- **Intent:** 1 sentence on the candidate's articulated reasoning or motivation

*Example:*
> When asked about driving results in a high-stakes environment, Ana described leading a market expansion project for a new product line in her previous role. **[Situation]** The team was behind schedule and a key supplier had withdrawn, threatening the launch timeline. **[Behavior]** Ana immediately convened a cross-functional task force, renegotiated contracts with two backup suppliers within 48 hours, and restructured the project plan with daily check-ins. **[Impact]** The product launched on the original date and exceeded first-quarter targets by 15%. **[Intent]** Ana shared that she viewed the setback as a signal to act faster rather than escalate — reflecting a strong bias for ownership and decisive action.

### Writing Principles

- **Behavioral and specific.** Every claim about the candidate must be supported by observable behavior. "Ana is a strong communicator" is not acceptable. "Ana structured her presentation with a clear thesis, three supporting arguments, and a decision recommendation — all completed within the allotted time" is acceptable.
- **Evidence-anchored.** Where possible, paraphrase transcript content in the report narrative.
- **Neutral and objective.** Avoid evaluative language like "impressive," "brilliant," or "disappointing." Describe what happened; let the rating communicate the judgment.
- **Consistent with ratings.** A Level 3 narrative should sound like a Level 3 narrative — not a Level 4. Calibrate the language to the rating.
- **No filler.** Avoid phrases like "overall," "in summary," "it can be noted that." Start paragraphs with the behavior, not the observation.
- **Use candidate's name.** Refer to the candidate by first name throughout (after the first full-name introduction).

### Overall Rating Guidance

The overall competency rating is holistic, not an average. Consider:
- Consistency across KBIs: did the candidate demonstrate the competency across all measured KBIs, or were some missed entirely?
- Depth of demonstration: at Level 3, behaviors are regular and reliable; at Level 4, they are notably above standard with clear impact on others
- Critical KBI weight: for some competencies, certain KBIs are more central. A candidate who misses the most critical KBI should not receive a 3 overall even if other KBIs are strong.
